## 1. ⭐ Synonyms and antonyms

**Method:** keep a notebook. **10 new words a day** with meaning, one synonym, one antonym and a sentence. Revise the whole notebook every Sunday. Over 12 weeks that is ~840 words — more than enough.

⭐ **Exam trick:** in a synonym/antonym question, three options are usually **synonyms of each other** and one is the odd one out. If asked for the *antonym*, the answer is the odd one; if asked for the *synonym*, the odd one is the distractor.

## 4. ⭐ Articles (a / an / the)

⭐ **Rule 1 — a/an depends on SOUND, not spelling.**
- **a** before a consonant *sound*: **a** university (yoo-), **a** European, **a** one-rupee note (wun-), **a** unit, **a** useful book
- **an** before a vowel *sound*: ⭐ **an** honest man (silent h), **an** hour, **an** heir, **an** MP (em-), **an** X-ray, **an** SMS

⭐ **Rule 2 — "the" is used with:**
- Unique objects: the sun, the moon, the earth
- Superlatives and ordinals: the best, the first
- ⭐ **Rivers, seas, oceans, mountain ranges, groups of islands, deserts:** the Ganga, the Gomati, the Bay of Bengal, the Himalayas, the Andamans, the Thar
- Names with "of": the Republic of India, the University of Delhi
- Newspapers, holy books: the Hindu, the Gita
- ⭐ **Plural country names:** the Netherlands, the USA, the UAE, the Philippines
- Musical instruments: play **the** flute
- Adjectives used as nouns: the rich, the poor
- Directions: in the north

⭐ **Rule 3 — "the" is NOT used with:**
- ⭐ **Single mountains and lakes:** Mount Everest, Betlingchhip, Lake Rudrasagar
- ⭐ **Most countries, states, cities, continents:** India, Tripura, Agartala, Asia
- ⭐ **Languages** (Bengali, Kokborok — *but* "the Bengali language")
- ⭐ **Meals, games, subjects:** have lunch, play cricket, study physics
- Abstract nouns used generally: Honesty is the best policy
- ⭐ Before proper nouns and before "both", "all" + possessive

## 5. ⭐⭐ Spotting errors — the top rules

⭐ **(a) Subject–verb agreement**
- ⭐ **Ignore words between the subject and the verb.**
  ✅ "The **list** of participants **was** displayed." (subject = *list*, not *participants*)
- ⭐ **`Each`, `every`, `either`, `neither`, `none`, `one of`, `everyone`, `nobody`, `somebody` take a SINGULAR verb.**
  ✅ "**Neither** of the two candidates **has** submitted…"
  ✅ "**Each** of the students **has** submitted **his** assignment."
  ✅ "**One** of my friends **has** invited me."
- **Two subjects joined by *and*** → plural. **But** if they refer to one person/thing → singular: "Bread and butter **is** my breakfast."
- ⭐ ***Either…or* / *Neither…nor*: the verb agrees with the NEARER subject.**
  ✅ "Neither the teacher nor the students **were** present."
- **With *as well as*, *along with*, *together with*, *besides*, *in addition to*** — the verb agrees with the **first** subject: "The manager, along with his staff, **was** present."
- Collective nouns (team, jury, committee) → singular when acting as a unit.

⭐ **(b) Tense**
- **Since/for + present perfect continuous** for actions continuing to now.
- ⭐ **After "If" in the second conditional, use "were" for all persons:** "If I **were** you…"
- ⭐ **No future tense after `if`, `when`, `until`, `as soon as`, `before`, `after`** (in time/condition clauses): "If it **rains**, I will stay." (not *will rain*)
- Past perfect for the earlier of two past actions.
- **"Had better", "would rather"** + bare infinitive.

⭐ **(c) Correlative conjunctions — memorise the exact pairs:**
📌 **Hardly … when** · **Scarcely … when** · ⭐ **No sooner … than** · **Not only … but also** · **Either … or** · **Neither … nor** · **Both … and** · **Such … that** · **So … that** · **Lest … should**

⚠ ✅ "**Hardly** had he reached the station **when** the train started." ❌ *than*
⚠ ✅ "**No sooner** had he arrived **than** it rained." ❌ *when*
⚠ ✅ "**Lest** he **should** fail" (not *lest he will not fail* — `lest` already means "in case… not").

⭐ **(d) Uncountable nouns take singular verbs and no plural -s:**
📌 **information, advice, furniture, luggage, baggage, equipment, scenery, machinery, poetry, news** (news is singular: "The news **is** good"), knowledge, work, traffic.
⚠ ✅ "He gave me some **advice**." ❌ *advices*

⭐ **(e) Nouns always plural:** scissors, trousers, spectacles, tongs, premises, alms, thanks, riches.

⭐ **(f) Double comparatives/negatives:** ❌ "more better", ❌ "most tallest", ❌ "hardly no one".

⭐ **(g) Adjective vs adverb:** "He runs **fast**" (not *fastly*); "She sings **well**" (not *good*).

⭐ **(h) Qualifying words / determiners:**

| Use | With |
|---|---|
| **Much, little, a little, amount of, less** | **Uncountable** nouns |
| **Many, few, a few, number of, fewer** | **Countable** nouns |
| **Some** | Affirmative sentences and polite offers |
| **Any** | Negatives and questions |

⚠ ⭐ **a few / few** and **a little / little**:
- ⭐ **"a few" / "a little" = SOME (positive)** — "He has **a few** friends" (he does have some)
- ⭐ **"few" / "little" = HARDLY ANY (negative)** — "He has **few** friends" (almost none)
- **"the few" / "the little" = all that there is**

⚠ **Between** (two) vs **among** (more than two). **Each other** (two) vs **one another** (more than two).

## 6. ⭐ Ordering of words and sentences

**Ordering of words in a sentence (jumbled sentence):**
1. Find the **subject** and the **verb** first.
2. Look for a capitalised word (usually the start) and the full stop.
3. Keep fixed pairs together: article + noun, preposition + object, auxiliary + main verb.
4. Standard English order: **Subject → Verb → Object → (Manner) → (Place) → (Time)**.
5. Read your answer back for sense.

**Ordering of sentences (para jumbles):**
1. ⭐ **Find the opening sentence** — it introduces the topic in full, uses no pronouns without antecedents, and no linkers like *however*, *therefore*, *this*, *such*.
2. ⭐ **Chain by pronouns and linkers** — a sentence starting with *this*, *these*, *he*, *it*, *such*, *however*, *moreover*, *therefore* must follow the sentence it refers to.
3. ⭐ **Follow chronology or logical sequence** (cause → effect, general → specific, problem → solution).
4. Look for **mandatory pairs** — two sentences that must be adjacent.
5. **Check the options first** — often only two or three orders are offered, so eliminating on the first sentence alone can settle it.

---

## 7. ⭐ Comprehension strategy

1. ⭐ **Read the questions first**, then the passage — you will know what to look for.
2. Read the passage **once, at speed**, marking the main idea of each paragraph.
3. Answer **only from the passage** — never from outside knowledge or opinion.
4. For **"the author implies/suggests"** questions, pick the option closest to the text; reject any option that is true in the real world but unsupported by the passage.
5. ⭐ **Extreme words** (*always, never, all, none, only*) in an option are usually wrong; **hedged words** (*may, some, often*) are usually right.
6. For vocabulary-in-context questions, substitute each option back into the sentence.
7. Do the **factual/detail** questions first, the **inference/tone/title** questions last.
8. Budget: ~1 minute for the passage + ~30 seconds per question.

## 8. ⭐ Quantitative aptitude formulas

### 8.1 Number system
📌 Divisibility: **2** (last digit even) · **3** (digit sum ÷3) · **4** (last two digits ÷4) · **5** (ends 0/5) · **6** (÷2 and ÷3) · **8** (last three ÷8) · **9** (digit sum ÷9) · **11** (alternating sum ÷11)
📌 **LCM × HCF = product of the two numbers**
📌 Sum of first n natural numbers = **n(n+1)/2**; squares = n(n+1)(2n+1)/6; cubes = [n(n+1)/2]²
📌 Number of factors of N = (a+1)(b+1)(c+1)… where N = pᵃqᵇrᶜ

### 8.2 ⭐ Percentage, profit and loss
📌 **Profit% = (SP − CP)/CP × 100** · **Loss% = (CP − SP)/CP × 100**
📌 **SP = CP × (100 + P%)/100**
📌 **Discount% = (MP − SP)/MP × 100**
📌 Successive percentage change: **x + y + xy/100**
📌 If a value increases by x% then decreases by x%, the net change = **−x²/100** (always a loss)

🔢 CP marked up 40%, then 25% discount: 100 → 140 → 105 → **5% profit**.
🔢 CP of 15 articles = SP of 12 → SP/CP = 15/12 = 1.25 → **25% profit**.
🔢 10% loss; ₹90 more gives 20% profit → 0.3×CP = 90 → **CP = ₹300**.

### 8.3 ⭐ Ratio, proportion, average, ages
📌 If a:b = x:y, then a = kx, b = ky
📌 **Average = sum/count**; for consecutive numbers, average = middle term (odd count) or mean of the two middle terms
📌 Weighted average = (n₁x₁ + n₂x₂)/(n₁ + n₂)
📌 **Alligation:** (Cheaper qty)/(Dearer qty) = (Dearer price − Mean)/(Mean − Cheaper price)

🔢 Average of 5 consecutive odd numbers is 27 → middle = 27 → numbers 23,25,27,29,31 → largest = **31**.
🔢 Ages 3:5; after 10 years 5:7 → 7(3x+10) = 5(5x+10) → 4x = 20 → x = 5 → A = **15**.

### 8.4 ⭐ Time, work, speed, distance
📌 **Work:** if A takes a days and B takes b days, together they take **ab/(a+b)** days
📌 Efficiency ∝ 1/time; M₁D₁H₁/W₁ = M₂D₂H₂/W₂
📌 **Speed = distance/time** · ⭐ **km/h → m/s: × 5/18** · **m/s → km/h: × 18/5**
📌 **Average speed for equal distances = 2xy/(x+y)** (harmonic mean)
📌 **Relative speed:** same direction = |a − b|; opposite = a + b
📌 **Train crossing a pole:** covers its own length. **Crossing a platform:** length of train + platform
📌 Boats: downstream = u + v; upstream = u − v; still water speed = (down + up)/2; stream = (down − up)/2

🔢 A: 12 days, B: 18 days → together = (12×18)/30 = **7.2 days**.
🔢 150 m train crosses a pole in 15 s → 10 m/s → 10 × 18/5 = **36 km/h**.

### 8.5 ⭐ Interest
📌 **SI = PRT/100**
📌 **CI: A = P(1 + R/100)ⁿ**, CI = A − P
📌 CI − SI for 2 years = **P(R/100)²**
📌 Under SI, a sum doubling in n years becomes 4× in **3n** years (interest earned must triple)
📌 Under CI, doubling in n years → 4× in **2n** years

🔢 ₹8,000 → ₹9,680 in 2 years SI → SI = 1680 → R = (1680×100)/(8000×2) = **10.5%**.
🔢 ₹10,000 at 10% CI for 2 years → 10000(1.1)² = 12,100 → CI = **₹2,100** (SI would be ₹2,000).
🔢 Doubles in 8 years at SI → 4× in **24 years**.

### 8.6 Permutations, combinations, probability
📌 ⁿPᵣ = n!/(n−r)! · ⁿCᵣ = n!/(r!(n−r)!) · ⁿCᵣ = ⁿC₍ₙ₋ᵣ₎
📌 P(E) = favourable/total · P(A∪B) = P(A)+P(B)−P(A∩B)

### 8.7 Mensuration
📌 Rectangle: area lb, perimeter 2(l+b), diagonal √(l²+b²)
📌 Square: a², 4a, a√2 · Triangle: ½ × base × height; equilateral (√3/4)a²
📌 Circle: πr², circumference 2πr
📌 Cube: volume a³, surface 6a² · Cuboid: lbh, 2(lb+bh+hl)
📌 Cylinder: πr²h, CSA 2πrh · Cone: ⅓πr²h, CSA πrl · Sphere: (4/3)πr³, SA 4πr²

## 9. ⭐⭐ Logical reasoning

### 9.1 ⭐ Number and letter series
Look for: constant difference · constant ratio · squares/cubes (±1) · alternating patterns · differences of differences · prime numbers.

🔢 3, 7, 15, 31, 63, **127** → 2ⁿ − 1 (double and add 1)
🔢 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, **42** → n(n+1); differences 4, 6, 8, 10, **12**
🔢 8, 27, 64, 100, 125 → all cubes except **100** (a square) — odd one out
🔢 1, 4, 9, 16, 25 → n²
🔢 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, **13** → primes

⭐ **Letter positions — memorise:** A=1, E=5, **I=9**, **M=13**, **N=14**, **O=15**, **S=19**, **T=20**, Z=26.
⭐ **EJOTY** trick: E=5, J=10, O=15, T=20, Y=25 — count from the nearest of these.
📌 **Opposite letter (reverse position) = 27 − position:** A↔Z, B↔Y, C↔X, M↔N.

### 9.2 ⭐ Coding–decoding
**Method:** compare letter positions of the code with the original.

🔢 MONDAY → NPOEBZ: each letter **+1**. So FRIDAY → **GSJEBZ**.
🔢 TEACHER → VGCEJGT: each letter **+2**. So STUDENT → **UVWFGPV**.
🔢 Reverse coding: CAT → ZXG (each letter replaced by its opposite, 27 − position).

Other types: number coding · substitution coding · conditional coding · symbol coding.

### 9.3 ⭐ Blood relations
**Method:** draw a diagram. Use **+** for male, **−** for female; horizontal line for spouse/sibling, vertical for parent–child.

🔢 "She is the daughter of my grandfather's **only son**" → grandfather's only son = the speaker's **father** → she is his **sister**.
🔢 "A is B's brother, B is C's sister, C is D's father" → A and C are siblings → A is D's **uncle**.
⚠ Watch for "**only son/daughter**" (identifies a unique person) and for gender assumptions — never infer gender that is not stated.

### 9.4 ⭐ Direction sense
Standard: **North up, South down, East right, West left**. Turning **right** goes N→E→S→W→N; **left** reverses this.
📌 Shortest distance = **√(net horizontal² + net vertical²)** (Pythagoras).

🔢 5 km north, right 3 km (east), right 5 km (south) → the north and south legs cancel → **3 km east** of start, **facing south**.
⚠ In "shadow" questions: in the morning the sun is in the **east**, so shadows fall **west**; in the evening the reverse. At noon the shadow is shortest.

### 9.5 ⭐ Ranking and ordering
📌 ⭐ **Position from right = Total − Position from left + 1**
📌 **Total = position from left + position from right − 1**
📌 Number of people between two positions = |difference| − 1

🔢 12th from the left in a row of 40 → 40 − 12 + 1 = **29th from the right**.

### 9.6 ⭐ Syllogisms
**Method:** draw Venn diagrams. ⭐ A conclusion is valid **only if it holds in EVERY possible diagram**.

📌 Standard results:
- All A are B + All B are C → **All A are C** ✅
- All A are B + Some B are C → **nothing definite** ❌
- Some A are B + Some B are C → **nothing definite** ❌
- All A are B + No B is C → **No A is C** ✅
- No A is B → **No B is A** ✅ (converts)
- Some A are B → **Some B are A** ✅ (converts)
- **All A are B** does NOT give "All B are A"

🔢 "All engineers are graduates. Some graduates are teachers." → **No definite conclusion** (those teacher-graduates need not be engineers).
⭐ **"Possibility" conclusions** are usually true unless the statements make them impossible.

### 9.7 ⭐ Seating arrangement and puzzles
Types: linear (single/double row) · circular (facing centre or outward) · square/rectangular · floor/box puzzles · scheduling grids.
**Method:**
1. Draw the frame and fix the **most definite** clue first.
2. Use a grid for who-does-what puzzles; mark ✗ as well as ✓.
3. ⚠ In circular arrangements **facing the centre**, "left" is anticlockwise and "right" is clockwise. Facing **outward**, this reverses.
4. Track "immediate neighbour" vs "somewhere to the left/right".

### 9.8 ⭐ Clocks and calendars

**Clocks:**
📌 **Minute hand moves 6°/minute; hour hand moves 0.5°/minute** (30° per hour)
📌 ⭐ **Angle between hands = |30H − 5.5M|** degrees (take 360 − θ if > 180)
📌 The hands **coincide 11 times in 12 hours** (22 times a day) and are **opposite 11 times in 12 hours**
📌 They are at right angles **22 times in 12 hours** (44 times a day)

🔢 At 3:00 → |30(3) − 5.5(0)| = **90°**.
🔢 At 4:20 → |30(4) − 5.5(20)| = |120 − 110| = **10°**.

**Calendars:**
📌 ⭐ **Odd days:** ordinary year = **1**, leap year = **2**, 100 years = **5**, 200 = 3, 300 = 1, **400 = 0**
📌 Leap year: divisible by 4; century years only if divisible by **400** (1900 was not a leap year; 2000 was)
📌 Day codes: 0 = Sunday, 1 = Monday, … 6 = Saturday

🔢 "Day before yesterday was Thursday" → yesterday Friday → today **Saturday** → tomorrow Sunday → day after tomorrow **Monday**.
⚠ Chain the days one step at a time; off-by-one is the standard error.

### 9.9 Other reasoning types
**Analogy** (A:B :: C:?) — identify the exact relationship first (part-whole, worker-tool, cause-effect, synonym, degree).
**Classification / odd one out** — find the shared property of the majority.
**Venn diagrams** — represent set relationships; count region by region.
**Data interpretation** — tables, bar/line/pie charts. 📌 In a pie chart, **1% = 3.6°**; a 90° sector = 25%.
**Statement–assumption / statement–conclusion / course of action** — an assumption is something **taken for granted**, not stated.
**Cause and effect · Non-verbal reasoning** (series, mirror/water images, paper folding, embedded figures) — note that Paper-I includes **spatial** ability under general aptitude.
**Cube and dice** — opposite faces of a standard die sum to 7; a cube painted and cut into n³ pieces gives 8 with three painted faces, 12(n−2) with two, 6(n−2)² with one, (n−2)³ with none.

## 13. ⭐ Indian Geography — essentials

**Physiography (6 divisions):** Northern Himalayas · Northern Plains · Peninsular Plateau · Indian Desert · Coastal Plains · Islands.
📌 Himalayan ranges north→south: **Trans-Himalaya, Greater Himalaya (Himadri), Lesser Himalaya (Himachal), Shiwaliks**.
📌 **Highest peak in India: Kanchenjunga** (Sikkim). K2 is in PoK/Gilgit-Baltistan.

**Rivers:**
- **Himalayan (perennial):** Indus (Sutlej, Beas, Ravi, Chenab, Jhelum), **Ganga** (Yamuna, Ghaghara, Gandak, Kosi, Son), **Brahmaputra** (called Tsangpo in Tibet, Siang/Dihang in Arunachal, **Jamuna** in Bangladesh)
- **Peninsular:** Godavari (⭐ **the longest peninsular river, "Dakshin Ganga"**), Krishna, Kaveri, Mahanadi (east-flowing, deltas) · **Narmada and Tapi** (⭐ **west-flowing, rift valleys, estuaries**)

**Climate:** tropical monsoon; SW monsoon (June–September) and NE/retreating monsoon (October–December, benefits Tamil Nadu). ⭐ **Mawsynram/Cherrapunji** — highest rainfall.
**Soils:** alluvial (most extensive and fertile), black/regur (cotton, Deccan), red, laterite, desert, mountain.
**Minerals:** ⭐ **Jharkhand, Odisha and Chhattisgarh** form the mineral belt — coal, iron ore, bauxite, mica.
**Agriculture:** Kharif (sown with the monsoon — rice, maize, cotton) · Rabi (winter — wheat, gram, mustard) · Zaid (summer). **Green Revolution** (wheat, Punjab/Haryana), **White Revolution** (milk, Operation Flood), **Blue Revolution** (fisheries).
**Administrative:** ⭐ **28 states and 8 Union Territories**. **Tropic of Cancer (23½°N)** passes through 8 states (including **Tripura**, near Udaipur). **82°30′E** is the Indian Standard Meridian (Mirzapur, UP).

## 14. ⭐ Indian Polity & Economy — essentials

**Constitution:** adopted **26 Nov 1949**, effective **26 Jan 1950**. **Dr B. R. Ambedkar** chaired the Drafting Committee. Borrowed features — parliamentary system and rule of law (UK), fundamental rights and judicial review (USA), DPSP (Ireland), emergency provisions (Germany), federal structure with a strong Centre (Canada), concurrent list (Australia), fundamental duties (USSR).

⭐ **Key parts and articles:**
- **Preamble** — sovereign, socialist, secular, democratic republic (socialist and secular added by the **42nd Amendment, 1976**)
- ⭐ **Fundamental Rights (Part III, Articles 12–35):** Right to Equality (14–18) · Freedom (19–22) · Against Exploitation (23–24) · Freedom of Religion (25–28) · Cultural & Educational (29–30) · ⭐ **Constitutional Remedies (32 — "the heart and soul of the Constitution", Ambedkar)**
- ⭐ **Article 21A** — Right to Education (86th Amendment, 2002)
- **DPSP (Part IV, 36–51)** — non-justiciable
- ⭐ **Fundamental Duties (Part IVA, Article 51A)** — 11 duties, added by the **42nd Amendment** (the 11th by the 86th)
- **Article 356** — President's Rule · **Article 370** (abrogated 2019) · **Article 371** — special provisions for several states including the Northeast
- ⭐ **Sixth Schedule** — autonomous district councils in Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram (directly relevant to Tripura's TTAADC)

**Union structure:** President (indirectly elected) · Vice-President · Prime Minister and Council of Ministers · **Lok Sabha (max 550)** and **Rajya Sabha (max 250)** · Supreme Court and High Courts · Election Commission · CAG · UPSC and State PSCs (⭐ **Articles 315–323** govern Public Service Commissions — worth knowing for the interview).

**Economy:** ⭐ **NITI Aayog** (2015, replaced the Planning Commission) · **RBI** (central bank, monetary policy, repo/reverse repo, CRR/SLR) · **GST** (2017, "one nation one tax") · Union Budget · fiscal deficit · **Census 2011** (next census pending) · poverty and employment schemes — **MGNREGA, PMAY, Ayushman Bharat, PM-KISAN, Jan Dhan Yojana, Skill India, Make in India, Startup India, Atmanirbhar Bharat**.

**Digital/IT policy (doubly relevant — Paper-II §13/§14 too):** ⭐ **Digital India** · **IT Act 2000** · ⭐ **DPDP Act 2023** · **Aadhaar, UPI, DigiLocker, UMANG, ONDC** (India Stack) · **BharatNet** · **IndiaAI Mission** · **India Semiconductor Mission** · **National Supercomputing Mission (PARAM)** · **CERT-In** · **MeghRaj** (GI Cloud) · **CSC (Common Service Centres)** · **e-District, e-Office**.
